Incident handler's journal

Google course exercises11 November 2026

Four entries about ransomware, packet analysis and file reputation, followed by notes on what I found difficult.

The journal

I used the course template to record the activity, tools, incident details and takeaways. Two entries cover supplied incident scenarios; the other two describe exercises with Wireshark and tcpdump.

Entries

Ransomware

I summarized a phishing-led ransomware attack at a health care company using the five W's. The scenario says the company shut down its systems. My notes cover the response stages and suggest disconnected backups, staff training and a response plan. I used no analysis tool for this entry.

Wireshark

I opened the supplied capture and inspected individual packets and their details. The amount of information was confusing at first; looking through the packets made it easier to follow.

tcpdump

I captured traffic in the terminal and tried filters. This was the hardest activity: I mistyped commands and got unexpected output. Checking the instructions and repeating the steps helped me finish it.

VirusTotal

I checked the SHA-256 hash of an email attachment in a financial services scenario. The hash and reported malicious reputation also appear in my phishing case note.

What I want to practise next

I'd like to inspect another packet capture and get more comfortable with command-line tools. The exercises also helped me see why an incident record needs clear notes and why a reputation result needs other evidence.

What's in the file

The journal contains four entries and a reflection, all dated 11 November 2026. It doesn't include packet captures, command output or tool screenshots.
